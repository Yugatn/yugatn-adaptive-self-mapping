# YASMP v2 — Adaptive Self-Mapping Protocol

**Author:** YUGATN  
**Status:** research / conceptual protocol  
**Version:** 2.0

## Purpose

YASMP is a subject-oriented framework for comparing several kinds of maps without confusing a map with the subject.

The protocol now separates five evidence layers:

1. Symbolic Self-Map
2. Behavioral Map
3. Sociometric Map
4. Context Map
5. Astronomical Context Map

The **Adaptive Self-Map** is a meta-layer. It records agreements, disagreements, uncertainty, changes over time and practical utility.

## Core rule

**Map ≠ Subject.**

A score, type, social position, behavioral pattern, symbolic profile or astronomical coordinate is not the subject.

## Evidence hierarchy

The protocol does not merge all inputs into one truth score.

Symbolic systems are hypothesis generators.

Behavioral and sociometric observations are empirical observations.

Astronomical ephemerides are physical calculations describing celestial state as a function of time and observer location. They are not psychological evidence by themselves.

## Protocol

### Phase 1 — Registration

Before interpretation, register:

- research question;
- population or individual case;
- observation period;
- variables;
- hypotheses;
- primary outcomes;
- exclusion criteria;
- planned statistical tests;
- known confounders;
- evidence status.

### Phase 2 — Baseline

Collect a baseline before exposing the subject to interpretive feedback.

The baseline may include:

- self-description;
- behavioral diary;
- validated psychometric measures;
- social-network or sociometric measurements;
- context variables.

### Phase 3 — Symbolic mapping

Generate the selected symbolic maps.

Each proposition must be stored separately rather than compressed into a single profile score.

Every proposition receives:

- source system;
- proposition text;
- confidence;
- subject rating;
- evidence status;
- later validation result.

### Phase 4 — Sociometric mapping

Record relationships using a predefined criterion.

Examples:

- preference;
- trust;
- cooperation;
- advice seeking;
- perceived support;
- conflict;
- communication frequency.

The criterion must not be changed after inspecting the result.

### Phase 5 — Context mapping

Record the circumstances of each observation:

- activity;
- location class;
- group;
- role;
- time;
- sleep or workload when ethically appropriate;
- major environmental events;
- subjective state.

### Phase 6 — Astronomical context

For each registered timestamp, optionally calculate an astronomical context record.

The record may include:

- UTC;
- local civil time;
- time scale;
- observer coordinates or privacy-preserving geographic cell;
- reference frame;
- Sun altitude;
- Moon phase and altitude;
- selected planetary positions;
- relevant astronomical events;
- ephemeris source and version.

JPL Horizons explicitly supports observer location, time settings, reference frames and multiple ephemeris types. See https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/manual.html and https://ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/doc/horizons.html.

### Phase 7 — Blind evaluation

Whenever possible, present symbolic propositions without source labels.

Collect:

- relevance;
- perceived accuracy;
- usefulness;
- emotional response;
- willingness to act;
- perceived specificity.

Compare the result against:

- random profiles;
- generic profiles;
- baseline self-description;
- evidence-based control measures where applicable.

### Phase 8 — Prospective validation

Predictions must be registered before the outcome occurs.

Each prediction contains:

- prediction;
- confidence;
- time horizon;
- falsification criterion;
- expected direction;
- observed outcome.

A prediction that cannot fail is not treated as a testable prediction.

### Phase 9 — Incremental-validity analysis

For astronomical variables, compare nested models.

**Baseline model:** calendar/time variables and relevant environmental controls.

**Extended model:** baseline plus astronomical variables.

The astronomical layer receives empirical value only if it provides reproducible out-of-sample improvement beyond the baseline.

No causal interpretation follows automatically from predictive improvement.

### Phase 10 — Adaptive revision

The final map contains:

- observed patterns;
- repeated patterns;
- failed hypotheses;
- context dependence;
- symbolic convergence;
- empirical convergence;
- conflicts;
- prediction performance;
- map utility;
- agency gain;
- residual uncertainty.

The map is revised after new evidence.

## Social superposition

A subject can occupy different relational positions in different groups or relationships.

Therefore the protocol treats social position as relational and context-dependent.

A subject may simultaneously be central in one network, peripheral in another, trusted by one subgroup and distrusted by another.

This is not necessarily contradiction. It can be evidence of contextual differentiation.

## Safety

YASMP must not be used as a sole basis for:

- medical diagnosis;
- legal decisions;
- employment exclusion;
- educational exclusion;
- credit decisions;
- relationship coercion;
- restriction of rights;
- automated judgments of human worth.

Every subject has the right to inspect, correct and withdraw their personal map where the implementation permits.

## Success criterion

The goal is not to prove a symbolic system.

The goal is to determine whether a map increases:

- self-understanding;
- choice;
- communication;
- development;
- autonomy;
- reciprocity;
- ability to select or modify an environment.

A map that increases compliance while reducing agency is not considered successful.
